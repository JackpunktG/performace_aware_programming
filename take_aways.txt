------ branch prediction

with a simple asm loop:
mov_cond_jump_asm:
    xor rax, rax
.loop:
    mov r8, [rdi + rax]
    inc rax
    test r8, 1
    jnz .skip
    nop
.skip:
    cmp rax, rsi
    jb .loop
    ret

we can test with differently filled buffers the cost of a jump and how much effect the branch prediction has.

    all 0's, min: 769606740 - ~0.2754sec 3.6313Gb/s,    *jnz never taken
		max: 803947144 - ~0.2877sec

	all 1's, min: 1436536052 - ~0.5140sec 1.9454Gb/s,   *always taken. cost of the jump halfing the throughput
		max: 1442267176 - ~0.5161sec

	2nd, min: 1201249616 - ~0.4298sec 2.3265Gb/s,       *taking half the time 
		max: 1257314044 - ~0.4499sec

	3rd, min: 1292757480 - ~0.4626sec 2.1618Gb/s,       *every 3rd
		max: 1384597900 - ~0.4954sec

	4th, min: 983855208 - ~0.3520sec 2.8405Gb/s,        *every 4th
		max: 1050534492 - ~0.3759sec

----- RAT 
Counterintuitively a set of instructions that are longer can sometime be executed quicker, due to the inst. out-of-order and parallelism execution of modern CPU's. To do with the RAT and Register files.

here are two asm loops;

RAT_add:
align 64
    mov rax, 1000000000
.loop:
    add rcx, 1     
    add rcx, 1
    dec rax
    jnz .loop
    ret

    add, min: 1311313752 - ~0.4692sec
		max: 1312704960 - ~0.4697sec

RAT_mov_add:
align 64
    mov rax, 1000000000
.loop:
    mov rcx, rax
    add rcx, 1
    mov rcx, rax
    add rcx, 1
    dec rax
    jnz .loop
    ret

  	mov+add, min: 1223584320 - ~0.4378sec 
		max: 1224189792 - ~0.4380sec

with the introduction for the mov inst. we break the dependancy chain of always needing to wait for the previous 'rcx' reg and instead build a two step dependancy chains of just the mov and add, which can be done in parallel.
*milage with the tests may vary. some people get up to a 2x speed boost, depending on CPU parallelisation specs.
